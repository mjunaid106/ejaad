Overview

A system of optimisation,
not just a diary.

Paper diaries, spreadsheets and basic practice software record appointments. Availo understands the constraints behind them and proposes a better plan — with reasons you can read and changes you approve.

  • 01

    Best slot recommendation

    For a new appointment, Availo ranks the top slots with practitioner, resource, times and human-readable reason codes. It never changes existing bookings to do it.

  • 02

    Daily schedule optimisation

    A deterministic solver re-plans the day across practitioners and resources to cut idle gaps and balance workload, then shows a before/after comparison.

  • 03

    Disruption recovery

    Cancellations, overruns and unavailable staff trigger a recovery proposal. Affected appointments get feasible alternatives — or an honest “no feasible alternative”.

  • 04

    Explainable reasons

    Every recommendation carries structured reason codes such as “fits an existing gap” or “preferred practitioner”. Explanations come from the score, not free text.

  • 05

    Human approval & audit

    Confirmed appointments are locked and never moved automatically. Staff accept or reject each change, and every applied change is traced to a run, an approver and a before/after value.

  • 06

    Utilisation analytics

    Practitioner and resource utilisation, idle minutes, conflicts and recovered capacity — with any revenue figure clearly labelled as an estimate.

How it works

Three things it does
exceptionally well.

Everything else is secondary until clinics see measurable value from these. The solver is the source of truth; no model ever writes an appointment directly.

1

Recommend

Rank the best slots for a new appointment under every hard constraint, with reasons.

2

Optimise

Re-plan an existing day to reduce idle time and balance workload, locked bookings fixed.

3

Recover

After a cancellation or delay, propose adjustments for the affected appointments.

  • No overlapping appointments for the same practitioner or resource.
  • Practitioner must hold every required skill tag.
  • Resource must hold every required capability tag.
  • Fits clinic operating hours, breaks and availability windows.
  • Required duration and mandatory buffers must fit.
  • Confirmed (locked) appointments are fixed inputs.
  • Every assignment belongs to the correct clinic tenant.
  • If infeasible, it says so — never an invalid schedule.

Generic by design

Built for any clinic,
not just dentistry.

“Dentist”, “chair” and “treatment” are configured labels, not hard-coded types. The engine works with practitioners, resources and services — so a new specialty is configuration, not a rewrite.

Practitioner

People & skills

Skill tags, working windows, leave and approved services. A service requiring a skill can never be assigned to someone without it.

Resource

Rooms & equipment

Capability tags, availability and maintenance exclusions. A service needing a capability only matches a resource that has it.

Service

What gets booked

Default duration, required skill and capability tags, and setup/cleanup buffers — all administrator-configurable.

Adding physiotherapy, veterinary or salon workflows means new tags, services and display labels — no changes to the scheduling engine. The system never decides what service a patient needs.

Trust

Minimal data,
strict isolation.

Availo keeps only what scheduling needs — and keeps each clinic's data to itself.

The patient record is minimal: a reference, name, contact number and a consent flag. There are no clinical notes in v1. Every record carries a clinic identifier, and queries require it, so one clinic can never read another's data.

  • Role-based access: receptionists book, practitioners see their own day, owners see the numbers.
  • Append-only audit log for configuration and appointment changes.
  • Configurable retention and a hard-delete workflow for erasure requests.
  • Explicit, recorded consent before any patient messaging; opt-out honoured immediately.
